# Design brief

## Job to be done

“When I hand over a recurring task, help the next person do it without needing me beside them.” The first release concentrates on everyday handoffs with visible steps and explicit choices. This is a product hypothesis, not a finding from user interviews.

## References consulted, November 16, 2026

- [Government Design Principles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/government-design-principles): organize around a concrete need and the full journey, including what happens outside the website.
- [FMHY’s AI prompts directory](https://fmhy.net/ai#ai-prompts) led to [DAIR.AI’s prompt-design guidance](https://www.promptingguide.ai/introduction/tips): replace vague style requests with specific constraints, examples, and testable behavior.
- [Are.na](https://www.are.na/): reviewed its plain-language product framing and content-first structure.
- [Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/): reviewed its website content and project-led framing. The attempted browser screenshot was blank; it was not used as verified visual evidence.

No third-party visual assets, testimonials, logos, or client claims were copied. No designer was personally consulted. Figma was not used for this release.

## Concrete direction

An instruction manual and a handover folder, translated to the web. Cobalt cover, chartreuse marker color, warm paper, ink rules, large tightly spaced type, and an original vector projector illustration. The hero’s paper object contains a real branch choice. Its purpose is to explain the interaction immediately.

The page progresses through a working example, a three-part explanation, specific starter guides, and a frank explanation of ownership and limits. The editor is quieter: a list of steps on the left, the selected instruction in the middle, route checks on the right. The reader shows one instruction at a time and retains a visible route history.

## Interaction constraints

Native buttons and form controls; visible keyboard focus; text equivalents for user photos; a skip link; no scroll hijacking, autoplay media, external fonts, background video, or decorative motion. The layout adapts at narrow widths. Reduced-motion preferences disable smooth scrolling. Automated width checks supplement screenshot review; they do not establish universal accessibility.

## Follow-up research

Observe someone unfamiliar with a task use a guide written by its owner. Measure where they pause, ask for help, or take the wrong branch. Use consented sessions before making usability or time-saving claims. Candidate next improvements should come from those failures, not from adding unrelated dashboard features.
